##  **Included in every Neon project, on every plan, by default.** Lakebase Postgres and the backend around it, with high availability and compliance built in.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/storage.21vg5_yb9su9t.svg)
### High availability.
Neon’s storage is [multi-AZ](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/high-availability) by default, without the need for HA standbys.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/branching.1bfdew5r0r2qh.svg)
### Branching.
Create [instant copy-on-write branches](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/branching) of your database, with auth, Object Storage, and Functions branching alongside it, for previews, tests, and agents.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/autoscaling.04_hxea2go9ps.svg)
### Autoscaling and scale to zero.
Compute [adjusts to load](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/autoscaling) and scales to zero when idle, including on the Free plan.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/connections.1gaamkbzgml04.svg)
### Connection pooling and read replicas.
Up to [10,000 pooled connections](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/connect/connection-pooling) per compute with built-in PgBouncer, and [read replicas](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/read-replicas) without added storage costs.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/extensions.2h-s3vpb1tcqh.svg)
### Postgres extensions.
Add extensions like [pgvector](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/extensions/pgvector), [PostGIS](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/extensions/postgis-related-extensions), [TimescaleDB](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/extensions/timescaledb), and many more.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/auth.44n-uawz--sbx.svg)
### Managed Better Auth.
[Authentication](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/neon-auth/overview) based on Better Auth that stores users in your database and branches with it.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/data-api.23e0t-dp63ww-.svg)
### Data API.
Query your database over HTTP with a [managed REST API](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/data-api/get-started), no backend server required.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/functions.40mr19pmluhra.svg)
### Object Storage and Functions.
S3-style [Object Storage](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/storage/overview) and [Functions](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/compute/functions/overview) in every project, with Free plan allowances, plus [Realtime](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/realtime/overview) in beta.
  * ![](https://neon.com/_next/static/immutable/media/security.161y81z0ndgm1.svg)
### Compliance.
Neon is ISO 27001 and 27701 certified, SOC 2 audited, and GDPR and CCPA compliant. HIPAA is available on Scale. See our [Security page](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=security).


## Your questions,   
answered
  * ### What does Neon include besides Postgres?
Alongside [Lakebase Postgres](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=lakebase), every Neon project can use [Managed Better Auth](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=auth), the [Data API](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/data-api/get-started), [Object Storage](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=object-storage), [Functions](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=functions), [AI Gateway](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=ai-gateway), and [Realtime](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/realtime/overview) (in beta). Together they form a backend that runs next to your database: Auth, Object Storage, Functions, and AI Gateway branch with it. Every service is available on every plan, including Free. A [project](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/manage/projects) in Neon is the top-level container for your environment. Each project includes all branches, with your Postgres databases and (if you deploy them) your Object Storage buckets, Functions, Managed Better Auth endpoints, and AI Gateway access.
  * ### What is a CU?
A CU (short for Compute Unit) is Neon's way of representing **Postgres database compute size**. Neon databases [autoscale](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/autoscaling), and CUs define how much CPU and memory your database is using at any moment. Each CU allocates approximately 4 GB of RAM, along with associated CPU.
  * ### What is a CU-hour?
A CU-hour is Neon's unit for measuring **Postgres database compute usage**. Because Neon databases scale to zero and autoscale, you're billed only for the database compute resources you actually use. In other words, your monthly database compute usage depends on:
    * How many compute endpoints you're running
    * How large your compute endpoints are (in CUs)
    * How long they run (in hours)
For a single compute, the calculation works like this:
`Monthly CU-hours = [average CU size] × [number of hours the compute runs in the month]` `Monthly compute cost = [monthly CU-hours] × [CU-hour price]`
The CU-hour price depends on your pricing plan:
    * Launch plan: $0.106 per CU-hour
    * Scale plan: $0.222 per CU-hour
Remember that when your database is idle, compute can scale down to zero, so you don't consume CU-hours while it's not running. [Learn more about Neon's billing.](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/about-billing)
  * ### What does 100 CU-hours per project give me on the Free plan?
On the Free plan, each project gets 100 CU-hours of **database compute** per month, and compute automatically scales down to zero when idle. What this means in practice:
    * You can create up to 100 projects, each with its own independent 100 CU-hour allowance
    * CU-hours are only consumed while a database is actively running queries. When a project is idle, compute scales to zero and uses no CU-hours
    * Usage is typically spread across short bursts of activity, not continuous runtime
A realistic example for a single project:
    * The database runs at 0.25 CU, the default compute size for Free plan projects, for ~12 hours per day. It scales down to zero the rest of the time
    * Over a month, this uses roughly 90 CU-hours (0.25 CU × 12 hours × 30 days), staying within the Free plan limits for that project
    * At 0.25 CU, 100 CU-hours covers about 400 hours of active compute, more than half the hours in a month
This model is designed to make it practical to have many small or intermittent databases (for development, demos, previews, or experiments) without being forced into an upgrade. [Learn more about our Free plan philosophy.](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=blog/why-so-many-projects-in-the-neon-free-plan)

  * ### How is Postgres database storage billed in Neon?
In Neon, you don't provision or manage database storage in advance. It scales automatically and invisibly as your data grows. At the end of each month, you're billed for the database storage actually consumed per project, measured in GB-months.
Neon bills storage usage using two separate metrics:
**History storage (or instant restore storage)**
**Instant restore** relies on Neon retaining a history of database changes so you can restore a branch to a previous point in time, create branches from past states, run Time Travel queries, and more. How long that history is kept is controlled by the **history window** on **Settings → Instant restore** (1 day by default on paid plans). See [History window](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/history-window).
**History** storage is billed based on the amount of Write-Ahead Log (WAL) retained within that history window, at $0.20 per GB-month on paid plans. This is billed separately from your regular database storage.
If you don't need deep recovery or long Time Travel, shorten the history window to reduce costs.
**Database storage (root and child branches)**
This is the storage used by your database data itself. Since Neon databases can branch, this is how branches contribute to database storage:
    * Root branches are billed based on their actual data size (for example, 5 GB)
    * Child branches _might_ be billed based on the minimum of: the accumulated data changes since the branch was created, or the underlying storage footprint, which is zero if the branch is still within the history window used for instant restore (in this case, the child branch effectively shares storage with its parent).
The sum of both components will be billed as your database storage at $0.35 per GB-month.
What this implies:
    * A child branch that has no data changes compared to its parent and is still within the [history window](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/history-window) does not incur additional database storage costs.
    * As a child branch accumulates changes over time, its storage usage increases.
    * If a child branch falls out of the history window, it becomes as expensive as a root branch, since it no longer shares storage with its parent.
To keep database storage costs low, keep child branches short-lived: set [expiration times](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/guides/branch-expiration), [reset them](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/guides/reset-from-parent) frequently, or delete branches when they're no longer needed.
  * ### How are extra branches billed?
Each paid plan includes a set number of branches per project at no additional cost (10 branches per project on Launch, 25 branches per project on Scale). You can create and delete branches freely within that allowance:
    * If the total number of concurrent branches in a project exceeds your plan's allowance, the extra branches are billed as branch-months, prorated hourly.
    * The price is $1.50 per extra branch-month (≈ $0.002 per hour)
    * You're only billed for branches that exceed your included limit, and only for the time they exist.
Example: The Launch plan includes 10 branches per project. If you already have 10 branches but create 2 additional branches and keep them for 5 hours each, that'd be 10 extra branch-hours total: 10 × $0.002/hour = $0.02
**How to avoid extra branch charges**
    * [Use branch expiration.](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/guides/branch-expiration) Set automatic deletion times on temporary branches so they're cleaned up when no longer needed.
    * Automate cleanup. Use the [Neon API](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/manage/branches) or [Neon CLI](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/guides/branching-neon-cli) to periodically delete unused branches and stay within your included allowance.
  * ### How can I control my Postgres database costs (compute and storage consumption)?
Database compute is often the most variable part of a monthly bill. For Neon databases, the most effective way to control compute costs is to configure maximum autoscaling limits and scale-to-zero.
Autoscaling limits act as a built-in cost ceiling: your database will never scale beyond the limit you set, even during traffic spikes. If you want to prioritize performance over costs in a particular compute endpoint (e.g. production), choose a higher limit. If you want to optimize for cost predictability, set a lower one. [Learn how to configure autoscaling limits.](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/guides/autoscaling-guide)
Another effective way to control database compute costs is to ensure scale to zero is enabled for all non-production branches. When a branch is idle, compute scales down automatically, so you don't pay for compute while it's idle. [Learn about scale to zero.](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/scale-to-zero)
To manage database storage costs, regularly clean up unused branches, snapshots, and projects, and avoid retaining a large history window for instant restore if your use case does not require it. [Learn more about optimizing storage usage.](https://neon.tech/unify?a=b9713199-772b-493e-b36f-63349a18199b&n=docs/introduction/cost-optimization)
